ChessHub

Free chess with no account and no server: six rated bots that run in your browser, a peer-to-peer game with a friend over a short code, and a game review that grades your moves and draws the engine's best line.

01

Six bots, one ladder

300 to 1850, calibrated to feel like the Chess.com bots rather than to be a FIDE rating.

Every bot is an alpha-beta search over chess.js, with the difficulty dialled by three things rather than one: how deep it looks, how much random noise it adds to its evaluation, and how often it deliberately plays a random legal move.

Rookie Robbie · 300
One ply, a wide ±260cp evaluation swing, and 28% of moves picked at random — hopeful rather than calculating.
Casual Carla · 600
One ply, 16% random moves, still happy to hang a piece.
Club Karl · 900
Two ply, 10% blunders. Punishes the obvious mistake.
Expert Emma · 1200
Two ply with quiescence, 5% blunders, much less noise.
Master Milo · 1500
Three ply with quiescence, 3% blunders, and a positional game.
Grandmaster Gus · 1850
Three ply with quiescence, 1.5% blunders, tie-break noise down to ±8cp.

Quiescence search is what makes the top of the ladder feel different: without it, a shallow engine stops counting in the middle of a trade and calmly hangs a piece. The whole search runs in the tab, so there is no network round trip between your move and the reply.

02

A friend, a code, no account

PeerJS does the matchmaking; the game itself travels directly between the two browsers.

Choose Online, create a game code, send it to a friend, and they join with it. There is no login, no lobby and no database of games — the two browsers connect over WebRTC through a public PeerJS broker, and every move goes straight down that data channel.

  • Host or join. The host creates a code; the guest enters it. Either side can disconnect cleanly instead of leaving a ghost game behind.
  • Both players have to be there. It is a live peer-to-peer game, not correspondence chess: there is no game history to come back to.
  • Your colour is yours. In an online game the board only accepts moves for your side, so you cannot move your opponent's pieces by accident.
03

Review, not just a hint

The same local engine that plays the bots grades the game you just finished.

The Analyse tab runs a Game Review at depth 3 over every move, compares what you played with what the engine preferred, and turns the difference into an accuracy figure and a move classification. An evaluation bar tracks the position as you step through the game, and a green arrow draws the engine's best line when you ask for it.

  • Step through it. The transport controls and the arrow keys walk the finished game while the eval bar and best line follow the position on the board.
  • Bring your own game. Load a PGN or a FEN from somewhere else, or copy the current game out as PGN.
  • Hint, mid-game. The Hint button runs the same depth-3 search without leaving the board.

This is a browser engine, not Stockfish. Three ply with quiescence is enough to catch the blunders that decide a club game; it is not an annotation of a grandmaster game.

04

A folder, not a stack

Two CDN scripts, one HTML file, one stylesheet and 539 lines of app code.

ChessHub is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. chess.js supplies the rules and legal move generation; PeerJS supplies the WebRTC connection. There is no framework, no bundler and no backend — GitHub Pages serves the repository root, and the whole game is the same files you can read.

git clone https://github.com/uxp70/chesshub.git
cd chesshub
python3 -m http.server 8000
# open http://localhost:8000

Opening index.html directly works too, though the peer-to-peer mode is happier over HTTP.
