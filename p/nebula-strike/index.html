Nebula Strike

A polished neon arena shooter that runs entirely in the browser, with a real account system, two leaderboards and wave-based combat. Static files, no backend, no build step.

01

Waves, bosses, upgrades

A top-down arena shooter built for the feel of a hit: screen shake, particles, power-ups and a level-up choice mid-fight.

Move with WASD, aim with the mouse, dash on a cooldown, and cut through waves of four enemy types with a boss every fifth wave. Power-ups and level-up upgrades change the run as it goes, and the soundtrack is synthesised with WebAudio — there are no audio files to download.

Difficulty
Rookie, Pilot and Ace, adjusting enemy health, speed and spawn rate.
Controls
Keyboard and mouse, touch (left thumb moves, right thumb aims and auto-fires) and standard gamepads.
Balance
A single CONFIG object at the top of js/game.js.
02

Accounts without a backend

Register, log in, log out or play as a guest — and the session survives a reload.

Passwords use a random 16-byte salt per account and SHA-256 over salt plus password, and are never stored in plain text. Sessions persist across reloads, profile stats travel with the account, and guest play is a first-class option that simply does not post to the leaderboard.

  • Everything lives under three localStorage keys: nebula_users_v1, nebula_session_v1 and nebula_scores_v1.
  • This is per-browser identity, which is the honest trade for a static site. The code marks the seam (TODO:online in js/auth.js) where Firebase or Supabase would slot in for cross-device logins.
03

Two leaderboards

One is yours. The other is nobody's server in particular — it is a public topic.

The global board publishes finished runs from logged-in players to a public ntfy.sh topic, and the tab aggregates the best score per pilot across everyone. ntfy keeps roughly twelve hours of message history, so "global" means the recent worldwide top — and every entry is validated on read, with junk dropped. Posts queue while offline and flush on reconnect.

  • Personal keeps your all-time bests, your last ten runs and your live global rank, in the browser.
  • Changing TOPIC in js/global.js moves or resets the board, since the first publish creates the topic.
  • The topic is public by design, so nothing secret belongs in it.
04

Squads over a public relay

Four pilots, four-letter room codes, and no server of your own to run.

The Squad tab (login required) creates or joins a room for up to four pilots, and the creator's browser hosts the run over a public MQTT relay — EMQX primary, HiveMQ as fallback, no accounts or keys. The host simulates; guests stream input at about 12 Hz and render snapshots at about 10 Hz.

  • XP and level are shared, the host picks upgrades for the squad, enemies scale with pilot count, and everyone keeps their own coins and credits while the shared score goes global.
  • If the host drops, the next pilot resumes from the latest snapshot — host migration, without a server to migrate.
  • If the relay is unreachable, solo play still works fully offline.
05

Two minutes to deploy

No install, no build, no environment variables.

Push the repository, set Pages to deploy from a branch, and the game is live at your Pages URL. A workflow at .github/workflows/pages.yml does the deploy on every push to main.

npx serve .
# or
python -m http.server 8000
# open http://localhost:8000
