Slipstream

A browser-based 3D arena shooter built around how it feels to move: Quake-style air control, auto-bunnyhop, dashes, slides and jump pads, in a neon arena with fully procedural audio. Solo or in a squad of up to four.

01

Movement is the game

Not "apply force while a key is held" — the Quake model, where momentum is something you keep rather than something you reset.

Everything else in Slipstream is arranged around the feel of the movement. Speed is the thing you spend and the thing you risk, so the camera sells it: FOV, roll, the wind layer and even the crosshair spread all respond to how fast you are going.

  • Projection-limited acceleration on the ground, so turning while moving re-projects your speed instead of throwing it away.
  • Air strafing — in the air only the component of velocity along your wish direction is limited, which is the whole reason you can gain speed by strafing in a circle. Gains taper near the cap so it stays controllable.
  • Auto-bunnyhop: holding jump re-jumps on landing, and the impulse is applied before ground friction, so a hop never bleeds momentum.
  • Forgiveness where it matters — 100 ms of coyote time off a ledge, 130 ms of buffered jump input, and jump-cut on release for short hops.
  • Dash and slide: a 1.65 s dash along your wish direction, airborne or grounded, with a FOV kick and trail burst; slide to duck under fire with its own lower friction.
02

Four weapons, five arenas

Archetypes that cover ranges, not upgrades of one another. Each keeps its own magazine and reload behaviour.

Switching is instant — 1–4, Q, the mouse wheel, or a tap on the touch strip — and each gun remembers its own ammo and reload state, so you can interrupt a shotgun reload with a rifle shot and come back to it.

VX-9 ARC
Hitscan automatic rifle, 30-round magazine, tight spread. The all-rounder.
BR-12 Breacher
Eight-pellet hitscan shotgun, six shells, one reload per shell — a big close-range burst you can cut short by firing.
LN-7 Lance
Precision rail: a single hitscan beam, five-round magazine, heavy at any range, slow cycle.
PR-4 Havoc
Splash rocket with radius damage and self-knockback — the rocket-jump tool, and a legitimate way to cross a gap.

Five hand-built layouts sit on the same movement kit: CIRCUIT (one wide atrium with a sunken centre and a ring of pillars), SPIRE (stacked platforms and pads over a deep shaft), RIFT (a long canyon with a raised span), DUSTFALL (a frontier main street) and GLACIER (a frozen basin of ice shelves and high bridges).

03

Events, not recolours

NEON SIEGE, FRONTIER and WHITEOUT each swap the arena, the enemy silhouettes, the guns and the light.

The point of an event is that it should read as a different fight rather than the same circuit in another colour. The trick is that the difference is render state, not geometry: colliders, spawns and jump pads never move, so the fight plays identically in every theme.

  • FRONTIER replaces additive neon edges with dark ones, turns glowing cap planes into solid roofs, adds a hard low sun and dust haze, and cuts bloom to a tenth — additive lines over daylight read as wireframe, not as a place.
  • The town is dressed like a real one: false fronts, porch roofs with painted shade, framed windows, boardwalks, barrels and lettered boards (SALOON, LIVERY, MERCANTILE, BANK…), all drawn into one canvas atlas rather than shipped as assets.
  • WHITEOUT is flat white overcast, drifted snow and pale ice with no glow; switching back to NEON SIEGE restores the chrome kit exactly, because the defaults live in one place and each event only states its differences.
  • Enemy skins change colour, emissive and silhouette only — health, speed, damage and score still come from the type and the difficulty tier, so a theme can never quietly change the balance.
04

Coins, bosses and squads

A progression layer that runs on static files, and co-op with no game server at all.

Kills and cleared waves pay coins, banked between sessions. The armory spends them on buffs for the next run only — buying is a plan for one fight, not a permanent stat — and each further stack of the same buff costs 60% more. Daily rewards climb a seven-day streak that resets if you miss a day.

  • Wardens arrive every fifth wave: one large hull with its own health bar, a thinner escort, a telegraphed five-shot volley and a repair pickup on death. It is one more row in the enemy table, so difficulty tiers, event skins and co-op snapshots need no special case.
  • Three difficulties — ROOKIE, STANDARD, OVERCLOCK — scale only levers the wave already has, so combos and scores stay comparable and the arenas stay readable.
  • Accounts are local: passcodes are stored as PBKDF2-SHA-256 hashes with a per-account salt (120k iterations, WebCrypto), never as text. It is device identity, not a cloud login — the game is static files, so it is a progression layer between sessions, and the seam a hosted provider would sit behind later.
  • Squad co-op uses WebRTC with the public PeerJS broker only for the handshake; after that every packet goes browser to browser. The host owns the wave, damage and score, guests simulate their own movement and report hits, and a downed player rebuilds after about 3.5 seconds instead of ending the run.
05

Under the hood

Static geometry batched to roughly twenty draw calls, audio synthesised at runtime, and touch treated as a first-class input.

Each arena's static geometry is merged into three batched meshes — bodies, edges and caps — with vertex colours and a single material, and every viewmodel is merged by material. The renderer adapts: AUTO lowers the render scale first and only drops bloom if that is not enough, while HIGH keeps bloom and MSAA and FRAMES pins resolution low for the smoothest motion.

  • All sound is Web Audio, generated at runtime: layered shots with per-weapon profiles, enemies panned by position, wind and ambience that respond to speed, and a procedural convolution reverb used as a send.
  • Touch goes through the same virtual-input layer as the keyboard, so the movement code has no mobile-specific branches — thumbstick, drag-to-look, hold-to-fire, a weapon strip and portrait FOV handling.
  • Tests cover movement, events, inventory, accounts, the armory, difficulty scaling, the boss cadence, a Playwright touch pass and two headless clients through the real broker.
